Early on a summer morning the beach below Colleville-sur-Mer is almost silent. The tide is out, the sand runs wide and ribbed towards a pale Channel, and the only sounds are oystercatchers and the wind in the marram grass. It takes a moment to connect this ordinary, lovely stretch of coast with what happened here. Then you climb to the bluff, see the long rows of white headstones above the beach, and the connection makes itself.

Normandy asks two very different things of a visitor. The landing beaches ask for attention and a certain quietness. Mont-Saint-Michel, a couple of hours down the coast, asks for good timing and respect for one of the most powerful tidal bays in Europe. Done well, a week here combines both, and it is one of the most moving trips you can make in France.

## Understanding the landing beaches

The Allied landings of 6 June 1944 came ashore along five sectors, known by their code names: Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno and Sword. The [Bayeux Bessin tourist office](https://www.bayeux-bessin-tourisme.com/en/go-to-bessin/d-day-normandy) lists them all, along with the sites most people come to see: the artificial harbour at Arromanches, the German artillery battery at Longues-sur-Mer, Pointe du Hoc, Sainte-Mère-Église and Pegasus Bridge. From a base in Bayeux, the office says these sites are all less than 45 minutes away.

Our advice is not to try to "do" all five beaches in a day. The coast is long, the villages are small, and the experience gets thinner the faster you go. Pick a theme instead:

- **The American sectors**: Utah, Pointe du Hoc and Omaha, ending at the American cemetery.
- **The British and Commonwealth sectors**: Gold, Arromanches, Juno and Sword, with Bayeux as your anchor.
- **The airborne story**: Sainte-Mère-Église at one end, Pegasus Bridge at the other.

Two themed days, with time to walk on the sand and read the memorial plaques in the villages, will stay with you far longer than a dash between car parks. A knowledgeable local guide adds a great deal, especially if you are tracing a relative's unit.

## Normandy American Cemetery

The [Normandy American Cemetery](https://www.abmc.gov/cemeteries-memorials/about-normandy-american-cemetery/) at Colleville-sur-Mer is run by the American Battle Monuments Commission. It holds 9,389 burials and commemorates 1,557 missing, across 172.5 acres. As [the ABMC's history page](https://www.abmc.gov/history/discover-the-history-of-normandy-american-cemetery/) puts it, the cemetery sits on a cliff overlooking Omaha Beach and the English Channel. A semicircular memorial colonnade, the Garden of the Missing and a circular chapel among the graves make up a landscape of great restraint.

Practicalities, from [the ABMC visit page](https://www.abmc.gov/plan/plan-your-visit-to-normandy-american-cemetery/):

- The cemetery is open 9am to 5pm daily, except 25 December and 1 January. Admission ends 30 minutes before closing.
- Admission is free and does not currently need booking.
- The path from the cemetery down to the beach has been closed since 2016 for security reasons, though the beach can be reached nearby.
- A section of the A13 motorway from Paris is due to close from 16 November 2026 to August 2027, so check your route if you are driving from Paris.

If you can, stay for the flag-lowering ceremony, which [the ABMC says](https://www.abmc.gov/cemeteries-memorials/about-normandy-american-cemetery/) is held one hour before the cemetery closes. Start in the visitor centre, which tells individual stories well, then walk out among the graves.

## Bayeux, its war cemetery and the tapestry

Bayeux is the natural base for the beaches, and its Commonwealth war cemetery is listed by the [Bayeux Bessin tourist office](https://www.bayeux-bessin-tourisme.com/en/go-to-bessin/d-day-normandy) as one of the region's key sites. It is quieter than Colleville and, to our minds, every bit as affecting. The cream headstones carry personal inscriptions chosen by families, and reading a few of them slowly is one of the most humbling things you can do here. If you are looking for a particular grave, the Commonwealth War Graves Commission's online records are the place to start before you travel.

One important change for 2026 and 2027: the [Bayeux Tapestry Museum](https://www.bayeuxmuseum.com/en/) is closed for renovation until autumn 2027. You can still explore the whole tapestry scene by scene on the museum's website, which is a fine way to prepare for a later trip. The town itself is still a pleasure: a cathedral that seems far too big for the streets around it, a little river turning old mill wheels, and good bakeries for a picnic before the beaches.

## Arromanches and the harbour

At Arromanches the remains of the artificial harbour still sit out in the bay: great concrete caissons, rusted and softened by decades of tides, scattered in a broad arc offshore. Seen from the clifftop at low tide, they show the scale of the operation better than any diagram. The village below is busy in summer, so arrive early or late in the day.

## Visiting with respect

These are cemeteries and places of real grief, and many visitors are families. The ABMC asks visitors to remember that [its cemeteries are sacred burial grounds](https://www.abmc.gov/plan/plan-your-visit-to-normandy-american-cemetery/) and to behave with respect and solemnity. In practice that means:

- Keep voices low and children close among the graves.
- Stay on paths and do not sit or lean on headstones.
- Ask yourself whether a photo is needed. A few quiet frames, never selfies, never posing.
- Dress as you would for any funeral or memorial: nothing elaborate, simply considerate.
- On the beaches, leave relics and debris where they lie.

## Mont-Saint-Michel: getting there and getting in

After the beaches, the drive south-west to Mont-Saint-Michel feels like moving into another century. The Mont rises from the flat bay like something drawn by a child, ramparts at its foot, abbey at its crown, and the first sight of it from the approach road is unforgettable.

You cannot drive to the Mont. According to the [Mont-Saint-Michel tourist office](https://www.ot-montsaintmichel.com/en/discover/visit-the-mont-saint-michel/access-the-mont-saint-michel/by-car-motorbike-and-camping-van/), the visitor car parks are about 2.7 km from the Mont, and your car park ticket includes the free Le Passeur shuttle that runs between the car parks and the Mont. Shuttle hours vary by season, with an on-demand night service, so check the current timetable on that page. Check the current car park charge there too.

Many people prefer to walk at least one way. The [tourist office's walking and cycling page](https://www.ot-montsaintmichel.com/en/discover/visit-the-mont-saint-michel/access-the-mont-saint-michel/by-foot-and-bicycle/) describes marked paths from the car park and a footbridge out to the Mont, shared by walkers and cyclists. Watching the Mont grow larger with every step is part of the experience.

Without a car, the [tourist office's train page](https://www.ot-montsaintmichel.com/en/discover/visit-the-mont-saint-michel/access-the-mont-saint-michel/by-train-plane-and-ferry/) says the nearest station is Pontorson, 9 km away, with a bus on to the Mont. There is also a direct bus from Rennes, which it times at 1 hour 10 minutes. Our guide to [getting around France by train and car](/posts/getting-around-france-trains-driving/) covers the wider connections.

The abbey at the summit is managed by the Centre des monuments nationaux. Book a timed ticket on the [official abbey website](https://www.abbaye-mont-saint-michel.fr/en/), where you can also check opening hours and the current ticket price. Our advice is to arrive early or late: the single main street fills by mid-morning, while the ramparts at dusk feel like a different place.

## Tides and safety on the bay

The bay is beautiful and dangerous. The [tourist office's tides page](https://www.ot-montsaintmichel.com/en/discover/our-essentials/the-high-tides-and-the-tidal-bore-a-great-spectacle-of-nature/) explains that the difference between low and high tide here can reach 15 metres, and that during high tides the sea withdraws 15 km from the coast and then rises very quickly. The strongest tides come 36 to 48 hours after full and new moons. The same page is blunt: it is extremely dangerous to venture alone into the bay, including close to the Mont.

So, in short:

- Never walk out onto the sands on your own, however inviting they look at low tide.
- If you want to cross the bay on foot, which is a wonderful experience, go with one of the [certified bay guides listed by the tourist office](https://www.ot-montsaintmichel.com/en/discover/visit-the-mont-saint-michel/the-mont-saint-michel-differently/the-crossings-of-the-bay-guides/).
- Check the [official tide times](https://www.ot-montsaintmichel.com/en/tide-schedules/) before you plan your day. Wind and atmospheric pressure can change the timing and height of the tide.
- To watch a big tide come in at the Mont itself, the tourist office suggests arriving two hours before the stated high-tide time.

## Where to stay

- **Bayeux**: the best all-round base for the beaches, with restaurants, a lively market and easy roads out to every sector. Staying in town also lets you see the cathedral lit up at night.
- **Arromanches and the coast villages**: for waking up within sight of the sea and walking the beach before anyone else arrives. Quieter in the evening, with fewer choices for dinner.
- **The Cotentin around Sainte-Mère-Église**: good if your focus is Utah Beach and the airborne story.
- **Near Mont-Saint-Michel**: stay at least one night on the mainland near the bay so you can see the Mont at dawn or after the day crowds leave. The villages and farms behind the bay are calmer than the hotel strip by the car parks.

## Before you go

- Check the [ABMC opening hours and travel notices](https://www.abmc.gov/plan/plan-your-visit-to-normandy-american-cemetery/), including the A13 closure.
- Look up the [tide times](https://www.ot-montsaintmichel.com/en/tide-schedules/) for your Mont-Saint-Michel day.
- Book your [abbey ticket](https://www.abbaye-mont-saint-michel.fr/en/) and check the current price.
- Check [Le Passeur shuttle hours](https://www.ot-montsaintmichel.com/en/discover/visit-the-mont-saint-michel/access-the-mont-saint-michel/by-car-motorbike-and-camping-van/) for the season.
- Pack layers and a waterproof. Normandy weather changes fast on the coast.
- Research any family connection before you travel, so you know which cemetery or memorial to visit.
