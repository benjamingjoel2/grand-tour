Walk out of Beaune on a September morning and within minutes you are among the vines. The rows run in tight green lines up a gentle slope, each block walled or marked with a small stone sign carrying a name that wine lovers know from labels. The soil underfoot is pale and stony, the air smells of crushed leaves and, at harvest time, of fermenting fruit drifting from open cellar doors. A tractor crawls along a lane; a church spire rises from a village that seems built entirely of honey-coloured stone.

Burgundy rewards the slow traveller more than almost any wine region in France. Its great names are tiny places, its producers are often families working small plots, and the pleasure lies in noticing how wines change from one village, even one slope, to the next. A week gives you time to do that properly.

## Why the vineyards are World Heritage

Since 4 July 2015, [the Climats, vineyards of Burgundy](https://www.climats-bourgogne.com/en/unesco-and-world-heritage_15.html) have been on the UNESCO World Heritage List. A *climat* is, in the words of the [Climats association](https://www.climats-bourgogne.com/en/what-is-a-climat_5.html), a specific vineyard site combining vine plots, grape variety and know-how, each with its own microclimate and geology. There are over a thousand named Climats along the thin strip of vineyards from Dijon to Santenay, which the association describes as running for 60 kilometres.

Once you understand this, the landscape starts to make sense. Those low stone walls, the little crosses and shelters among the vines, the names painted on gateposts: they mark centuries of careful observation about which patch of slope gives which kind of wine.

## Côte de Nuits: the red heartland

The northern half of the Côte d'Or runs south from Dijon. According to the [Burgundy wine board (BIVB)](https://www.bourgogne-wines.com/wine-and-terroir/our-vineyards/cote-de-nuits-and-hautes-cotes-de-nuits/cote-de-nuits-and-hautes-cotes-de-nuits-purple-and-gold,2470,9313.html), the Côte de Nuits vines stretch over 20 km between Dijon and Corgoloin, in places just 200 metres wide. Pinot Noir dominates, and most of the region's grands crus are red.

The village names read like a wine list: Gevrey-Chambertin, Morey-Saint-Denis, Chambolle-Musigny, Vougeot, Vosne-Romanée, Nuits-Saint-Georges. Our favourite way to see them is slowly, stopping to walk up through the vines above each village, where the slope steepens and the views open across the plain. The walled Clos de Vougeot, with its château standing alone among the vines, is the classic photograph, and worth the stop.

Above the main slope, the Hautes Côtes de Nuits plateau is quieter, wilder and good for walking, with farms, woods and scattered vineyards.

## Côte de Beaune: whites, and Beaune itself

South of Nuits, the [Côte de Beaune](https://www.bourgogne-wines.com/wine-and-terroir/our-vineyards/cote-de-beaune-and-hautes-cotes-de-beaune/cote-de-beaune-and-the-hautes-cotes-de-beaune-a-winegrowing-region-renowned-for-its-legendary-wines,2471,9314.html) runs about 20 km from Ladoix-Serrigny to the Maranges hillsides. The BIVB explains that Pinot Noir leads around Beaune, with Pommard and Volnay known for their reds, while from Meursault southwards Chardonnay takes over. The hill of Corton, rising above Aloxe-Corton, produces both red and white grands crus.

This is where many visitors fall for white Burgundy. Meursault, Puligny-Montrachet and Chassagne-Montrachet are small, quiet villages that you can walk round in half an hour, but the wines they give their names to are among the most celebrated whites in the world.

## Beaune and the Hospices

Beaune is the wine capital, a walled town of cobbled lanes, wine shops and a market square that fills on market mornings. Its great sight is the Hôtel-Dieu of the Hospices de Beaune, founded in 1443 by Nicolas Rolin, Chancellor of Burgundy, and his wife Guigone de Salins, according to the [Hospices Civils de Beaune](https://www.hospices-de-beaune.com/). The institution still runs the town's hospital today, and the old Hôtel-Dieu is now a historic monument.

Step inside the courtyard and you see the famous polychrome roofs of glazed tiles. The [Beaune tourist office](https://www.beaune-tourisme.fr/profiter/patrimoine-et-culture-savoirfaire/hotel-dieu-hospices-de-beaune-beaune-fr-3860146/) highlights the Grande Salle des Pôvres, the great hall of the old hospital, and Rogier van der Weyden's Last Judgement polyptych. Opening hours change by season and online booking is available, so check the current times and ticket price on that page.

The Hospices also own vineyards, and their wines are sold at a celebrated charity auction held, as [the tourist office notes](https://www.beaune-tourisme.fr/explorer/decouvrir-les-hospices-de-beaune/), every year on the third Sunday of November. Beaune is very busy that weekend; book a room far ahead or choose another time.

For a primer before your first tasting, the [Cité des Climats et vins de Bourgogne](https://www.bourgogne-wines.com/trips-to-the-bourgogne-region/cite-des-climats-et-vins-de-bourgogne/the-cite-des-climats-et-vins-de-bourgogne,3497,18145.html) has sites in Beaune, Chablis and Mâcon. The Beaune site has a dedicated space on the Climats.

## Dijon

Dijon deserves at least a full day. It is an elegant, walkable city of ducal palaces, mustard shops and tiled roofs, and the place where the Route des Grands Crus begins. The [Cité internationale de la gastronomie et du vin](https://www.destinationdijon.com/en/stay/weve-tested-it-for-you/the-international-centre-for-gastronomy-and-wine/) occupies the city's former general hospital, with exhibitions, tastings and workshops, shops and restaurants; the Dijon tourist office notes that entry is free. Pair it with the covered market in the morning and a long lunch.

## Chablis: a day trip north

Chablis feels like another region. The [BIVB](https://www.bourgogne-wines.com/wine-and-terroir/our-vineyards/chablis-and-the-grand-auxerrois/chablis-and-grand-auxerrois-the-green-gold-of-the-bourgogne-region,2469,9312.html) places it midway between Beaune and Paris, with vineyards on the hillsides of the Serein valley and Chardonnay used for all its wines. Tasting through the four levels, Petit Chablis, Chablis, Chablis Premier Cru and Chablis Grand Cru, is a lesson in how the same grape changes with site. The little town has its own Cité des Climats site, and it makes a natural stop if you are arriving from or returning to Paris.

## A week, roughly sketched

- **Day 1**: Dijon, the market and the Cité de la gastronomie.
- **Day 2**: The Côte de Nuits, village by village, along the [Route des Grands Crus](https://www.beaune-tourisme.fr/explorer/la-route-des-grands-crus/), which runs from Dijon to Santenay.
- **Day 3**: Beaune: the Hospices, the Cité des Climats, cellars in town.
- **Day 4**: Cycle the Côte de Beaune on the Voie des Vignes (see below).
- **Day 5**: The Hautes Côtes: plateaux, woods and a slower pace.
- **Day 6**: Chablis, on your way north.
- **Day 7**: A spare day for a return visit to a favourite village, or simply a long lunch.

## By bike: the Voie des Vignes

The best way to see the Côte de Beaune is under your own power. The [Voie des Vignes](https://www.beaune-tourisme.fr/explorer/nature-et-randonnee/la-voie-des-vignes/) is a cycle route with a smooth asphalt surface, running 22 km from Beaune to Santenay through Pommard, Volnay, Meursault, Puligny-Montrachet and Chassagne-Montrachet, with a 45 km link north to Dijon. An e-bike makes the gentle climbs effortless. Spit at tastings if you are riding, and see our guide to [cycling in France for beginners](/posts/cycling-in-france-for-beginners/) for the basics.

## Visiting domaines politely

Burgundy's domaines are mostly working family estates, not visitor centres. A few habits will make you welcome:

- **Book ahead.** Contact estates in advance rather than turning up unannounced. Estates carrying the [Vignobles & Découvertes label](https://www.bourgogne-wines.com/trips-to-the-bourgogne-region/the-vignobles-decouvertes-cellars,2568.html) have committed to a friendly welcome in French and other languages, and are a good place to start.
- **Go easy on small producers at harvest time.** When the grapes are coming in, the whole family may be in the vines all day.
- **Learn a little French.** A greeting and a thank-you go a long way.
- **Spit, and do not feel shy about it.** Spittoons are there to be used.
- **Buy if you can.** You are never obliged to, but for a small producer a purchase is the natural thanks for their time.
- **Skip the perfume.** Strong scents get in the way of everyone's tasting.

## Drinking and driving

This matters in Burgundy more than almost anywhere. Under French law, as set out on the government's [Service-Public site](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F2881?lang=en), it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol level of 0.5 g per litre or more, and the limit falls to 0.2 g per litre for probationary licence holders. The [UK government's France travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/france/safety-and-security) notes that the French limit is a third lower than in England and Wales and that police carry out regular roadside checks.

Our advice is simple: the driver spits every time, or you plan days on foot, by bike, or with a guide who drives.

## Where to stay

- **Beaune**: the obvious base, central to both Côtes, full of restaurants and wine shops, and walkable in the evening. Busy in summer and on the Hospices auction weekend.
- **A Côte de Nuits village** such as Gevrey-Chambertin or Nuits-Saint-Georges: quieter evenings, vines at the door, and easy access to Dijon.
- **A Côte de Beaune white-wine village** such as Meursault or Puligny-Montrachet: peaceful, close to the Voie des Vignes, ideal for cycling days.
- **Dijon**: best if you arrive by train and want city life, with day trips south.
- **Chablis**: one night here breaks the journey to or from Paris.

## Before you go

- Book domaine visits ahead, and plan around harvest time.
- Check [Hôtel-Dieu opening hours](https://www.beaune-tourisme.fr/profiter/patrimoine-et-culture-savoirfaire/hotel-dieu-hospices-de-beaune-beaune-fr-3860146/) and the current ticket price.
- Decide who drives each day, and know the [French alcohol limits](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F2881?lang=en).
- Pack a wine carrier or plan shipping for bottles you buy.
- Bring comfortable shoes: cellars are cool and the best views are up the slope.
