The first thing you notice is the smell. Step off the path into the scrub and the heat pulls a scent out of the maquis that is part rosemary, part resin, part something wild and nameless. Then the views catch up with you: granite ridges stacked one behind another, pine forests dropping into gorges, and far below, the sea, impossibly blue. Corsica has been called the mountain in the sea, and on foot you understand why. Few places in Europe pack so much wild country so close to the beach.

It is also a place that demands respect. The mountains are high and rugged, summer heat is fierce, water can be scarce, and fire is a constant concern. Plan well, though, and Corsica gives walkers some of the finest days on foot anywhere in France, whether you are tackling a legendary long-distance trail or strolling a coastal path before a swim.

## The GR20: the big one

The GR20 crosses the island's mountain spine from Calenzana in the north-west to Conca in the south. The [Parc naturel régional de Corse](https://www.pnr.corsica/fr/randonnees-decouverte/gr-20-fra-li-monti), which manages its refuges, describes it as one of the most difficult trails in Europe, and it is not exaggerating. The park divides the route into 16 stages, roughly 15 days of walking.

The trail falls into two halves either side of Vizzavona, a forest village with hotels, gîtes and supplies, and a station on the island's railway:

- **The north (stages 1 to 9, Calenzana to Vizzavona)** is the rougher, more technical half: steep rock, scrambles and long, punishing days.
- **The south (stages 10 to 16, Vizzavona to Conca)** is less technical but still demanding, with some very long stages.

If you have neither the time nor the appetite for the whole thing, walking just one half is a perfectly respectable goal, and an excellent introduction.

A few rules the park highlights that catch people out:

- On stage 4, the old route through the Cirque de la Solitude has been closed to the public since an accident in 2015. The diversion is now the official route.
- From Calenzana, under a municipal order, you may not set off on the GR20 after 11:00.
- Early in the season, snow can linger on stage 7, which may require crampons, a rope and an ice axe.
- Swimming in the high mountain lakes is banned.

### Booking the refuges

This is the crucial bit. According to the park, [booking is mandatory](https://www.pnr.corsica/fr/randonnees-decouverte/gr-20-fra-li-monti) for beds, bivouac pitches and bunk places at the GR20 refuges, through the park's booking platform, [pnr-resa.corsica](https://pnr-resa.corsica). Even if you carry your own tent, you must book one bivouac place per person, because wild camping is banned and bivouacking is permitted only near the refuges or in designated areas.

Other details worth knowing from the park's page:

- In 2026 the refuges were staffed from 16 May to 4 October; check the park's site for next season's dates before you plan.
- Bring your booking confirmation to show the warden on arrival.
- Meals are ordered on site, not in advance, and your reserved place is held until the evening, so do not dawdle.
- Bookings are tied to a specific refuge on a specific date, so skipping or doubling up a stage may cost you your reservation.
- Check current prices and accepted payment methods on the park's page before you go.

Our advice: book the moment the season opens, decide your direction and pace honestly, and build in a rest day at Vizzavona if you are walking the full route.

## Gentler long-distance routes: Mare e Monti and Mare a Mare

Not every walker wants a fortnight of rock and refuges. The park maintains a family of [lower, friendlier long-distance trails](https://www.pnr.corsica/fr/randonnees-decouverte) that link villages, with nights in gîtes rather than mountain huts. These are, to our mind, the most underrated walking in Corsica: you see real village life, eat better, and still get big views.

- **[Mare e Monti](https://www.pnr.corsica/fr/randonnees-decouverte/mare-e-monti)** ("sea and mountains") runs from Calenzana to Cargèse along the west coast: 121 km over 10 days, with stages of four to six and a half hours. The park says it has no particular difficulties, though some stages are long and call for fitness. The trail runs above the sea while staying close to the high peaks, and the park recommends spring and autumn; in summer, start early and carry plenty of water.
- **[Mare e Monti Sud](https://www.pnr.corsica/fr/randonnees-decouverte/mare-e-monti-sud-r)** links Porticcio and Propriano: 66 km over 5 days, described as accessible to all.
- **[Mare a Mare Nord](https://www.pnr.corsica/fr/randonnees-decouverte/mare-a-mare-nord)** crosses the island from Moriani on the east coast to Cargèse on the west, via Corte: 139 km over 11 to 12 days. Because it climbs high, the park says it is only walkable from mid-April to November. Note a current diversion: bridge works at Muricciolu (Albertacce) have diverted the Mare a Mare Nord and the Sentier de la Transhumance, with detours marked by orange circles.
- **[Mare a Mare Sud](https://www.pnr.corsica/fr/randonnees-decouverte/mare-a-mare-sud-r)** runs from Porto-Vecchio to Propriano: 73 km over 5 days. The park calls it a family-friendly mid-mountain walk suitable for less experienced walkers, walkable all year, with tree cover that takes the edge off the summer heat. Accommodation is booked through the same pnr-resa.corsica platform.

If this is your first walking trip in Corsica, Mare a Mare Sud or Mare e Monti Sud is where we would start.

## Coastal and day walks

You do not need to walk for a week to love Corsica on foot. Some of the island's best walking comes in two-hour doses with a beach at the end. The island's tourism agency, Visit Corsica, picks out [five coastal nature trails](https://www.visit-corsica.com/en/Explore-Corsica/Our-inspirations/Inspirations-Sea-and-rivers/Wellness-walks-by-the-sea-where-to-recharge-your-batteries-in-Corsica), and they make a lovely shortlist:

- **The Bruzzi trail at Pianottoli-Caldarello,** a loop of about two hours with no significant climbing, through cork oaks and wind-sculpted maquis, with views to the Bruzzi islands and Sardinia.
- **The Revellata peninsula near Calvi,** following the old customs path, with views over the bay and the open sea.
- **Punta di Columbara at Tamaricciu beach,** an hour-long loop across dunes and scrubland.
- **Bonifacio's trails,** including the Campu Rumanilu path, which starts near the Saint-Roch chapel with a stone staircase carved into the limestone.
- **The mouth of the Fangu,** a delta walk in a biosphere reserve on the west coast.

Inland, the valleys around Corte and the forests of Vizzavona are full of half-day walks to waterfalls, pools and viewpoints; the local tourist offices are the best place to ask what is open and suitable on the day.

## Fire risk and massif closures

This is the part of Corsican walking that visitors most often overlook, and it matters. The [Corse-du-Sud prefecture](https://www.corse-du-sud.gouv.fr/Actions-de-l-Etat/Agriculture-foret/La-prevention-des-feux-de-foret/Emploi-du-feu/Carte-du-risque-d-incendie-pour-les-activites-de-pleine-nature) explains that from 15 June to 30 September it is forbidden to smoke or light fires in natural areas, and it publishes a daily fire-risk map for outdoor activities.

The [fire-risk map for Corsica](https://www.risque-prevention-incendie.fr/corse/) is updated at 18:00 for the following day and colour-codes the island's main massifs:

- **Yellow:** be careful and stay alert.
- **Orange:** limit your time there and leave before 11:00.
- **Red:** do not enter.
- **Black:** access is forbidden.

Some massifs may be closed to the public at any time of day when the risk is severe. Check the map every evening during the summer, plan your next day around it, and keep a plan B. The prefecture also issues specific orders for particular sites, so check its news pages before you go.

## Season, heat and water

The park's advice for its gentler routes is spring and autumn, and we agree for almost all walking here. In midsummer the heat on exposed slopes is brutal by late morning, the fire restrictions bite, and the trails and refuges are at their busiest.

Whatever the season:

- **Start early.** Be walking at first light and aim to be off exposed ground by early afternoon.
- **Carry more water than you think.** The park warns that there is no water on parts of the GR20's early stages, and that stage 12 suffers shortages in July and August. Carry a filter or purification tablets: both the park and [Visit Corsica's GR20 safety advice](https://www.visit-corsica.com/en/Explore-Corsica/What-s-up/An-inclusive-and-carefree-summer-in-Corsica/Hike-on-the-GR20-safely) say to treat or filter water before drinking it.
- **Respect the weather.** The park points out that storms can build fast, even in summer. Check [Météo-France's mountain forecast](https://meteofrance.com/meteo-montagne) and turn back if conditions worsen.
- **Do not walk alone in the high mountains,** stay on marked trails, and tell someone your plans. The European emergency number is 112, but mobile coverage is unreliable near several GR20 refuges.

## Where to stay

For walkers without a long-distance plan, two or three bases cover a lot of ground:

- **Corte.** The island's mountain heart, on the central railway line, close to the Restonica gorge and on the route of the Mare a Mare Nord. The best base for inland day walks.
- **Vizzavona.** A forest village on the railway, right on the GR20 at its halfway point. Ideal for a few quiet nights among the pines, with walks from the door.
- **Calvi or the Balagne.** The north-west combines coastal walks like the Revellata with access to the start of the GR20 and Mare e Monti at Calenzana, and beaches for rest days.
- **Porto-Vecchio, Propriano or Bonifacio.** In the south, these put you within reach of Mare a Mare Sud, Mare e Monti Sud and the coastal trails.

Getting around without a car is possible to a point: the [Chemins de fer de la Corse](https://cf-corse.corsica/les-gares/) run a central line between Bastia and Ajaccio, with stations including Corte and Vizzavona, and a second line serving the Balagne, including Calvi and Ile Rousse. For trailheads elsewhere, you will want a car. ## Before you go

- **GR20:** book every refuge night (bed, bunk or bivouac) on pnr-resa.corsica as early as possible, and check the coming season's dates on the park's site.
- **Gîte routes:** check stage lengths and current diversions on each route's park page, and book accommodation ahead.
- **Fire risk:** from mid-June, check the fire-risk map every evening at 18:00 for the next day.
- **Weather:** check Météo-France's mountain forecast before each walk.
- **Water:** carry plenty, plus a filter or purification tablets.
- **Gear:** good boots, a waterproof, a warm layer, sun protection, a headtorch and a first-aid kit, even in summer.
- **Rules:** no wild camping, no fires or smoking in natural areas in summer, and no swimming in the high mountain lakes.
- **Emergencies:** save 112 in your phone, and do not rely on having signal.
